The Discipline of Deep Work

By Abhi Sharma | Published August 24, 2026

Deep work is the ability to focus without distraction on a cognitively demanding task. It is a skill that grows rarer even as it grows more valuable in our economy for a simple reason: the world is increasingly filled with things that fight for your attention.

Consider the difference between a student who studies with their phone in another room versus one who keeps it nearby, glancing at it every few minutes. The former enters a state of flow far more easily, producing better work in less time.

"The ability to concentrate intensely is a skill that must be trained, much like a muscle."

Building this skill requires deliberate practice over time. It is not enough to simply want to focus; one must structure the environment to make distraction difficult and focus the default state.

Many professionals block out specific hours in their calendar dedicated entirely to uninterrupted work, treating these blocks with the same seriousness as an important meeting.

Ultimately, the capacity for deep work compounds. Each session strengthens the ability to enter focus more quickly the next time, creating a virtuous cycle of increasingly productive output.
